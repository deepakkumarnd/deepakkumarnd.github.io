# {post_title}

Snowy is my dog, whom we brought home as a puppy after finding him on a nearby street. He was the weakest among a litter of puppies that had been abandoned by someone. Yet, there was something about his adorable, cat-like features that instantly caught my attention. I felt drawn to him and decided to take him home. I placed him in a cardboard box and brought him along. He appeared to be a mixed breed, with features resembling a cross between an Indian Pariah Dog and a Terrier. A quick search using Google Lens seemed to confirm my guess!

We brought him home in January 2026. Judging by his size and appearance, I estimated that he was somewhere between two weeks and a month old. His tiny, shivering body was covered in countless ticks, and his stools consisted mostly of worms. Somehow, this little creature had managed to survive until then, despite being so frail that he could barely even walk. To be honest, I wasn't sure he would survive another week.

I started by giving him water, small pieces of boiled egg, milk diluted with plenty of water, and puppy food from a nearby pet store. I also took him to a nearby veterinary clinic for treatment. Over the next couple of weeks, I made several trips to the clinic, carrying him each time. His deworming treatment was completed, and I applied a small amount of spot-on treatment to get rid of the ticks. It worked like magic! Within the next 24 hours, Snowy became noticeably more active, and the ticks and worms were no longer a problem. He slowly began to play a little, although he still spent most of his time sleeping.

After about a week, Snowy developed a cough. It sounded as though something was stuck in his throat, making him cough repeatedly in an attempt to clear it. By the following day, the coughing had become almost continuous and sounded quite alarming. I searched online for possible causes, and the symptoms seemed similar to kennel cough, which made me quite worried. I took him to the veterinary clinic, but the doctor ruled out kennel cough. He gave Snowy an antibiotic injection and asked me to bring him back the following day. We continued with the injections for the next three days.

The biggest challenge during this period was that Snowy refused to eat anything. He would simply lie down, barely moving, and continue coughing. With no other option, I had to feed him using a syringe, giving him liquid food and, at times, just glucose water to keep him going. By the fourth day, his coughing had started to subside. He gradually regained his appetite, began eating solid food again, and finally showed encouraging signs of recovery.

Snowy also had a few visitors during this time. A big dog would often drop by, take a look at him, and seem to check if he was doing alright. Snowy would excitedly run towards him, and the two would exchange a few barks and affectionate touches before the big dog went on its way. It was almost like watching a father visit his son, making sure he was safe and doing well before leaving again.

Snowy grew quickly, and I started teaching him a few basic commands. He soon learned to sit and fetch things whenever I threw them. I used to give him a bath once a week, but keeping him clean was quite a challenge. He would often run off to join a pack of stray dogs and return covered in dirt. Sometimes, he would disappear for so long that it became difficult to find him, and I had to go searching for him. On a few occasions, we even received calls from concerned neighbours. Snowy loved chasing other dogs and would sometimes run alongside passing bikes and rickshaws. Some dog-loving neighbours who knew me would bring him back home, while others would keep him safely inside their gated compounds and feed him. Unfortunately, not everyone was kind to him; some even threw stones at him. Eventually, I decided to buy a chain, and Snowy was finally put on a leash to keep him from wandering off.

The old visitors have now been replaced by a few young dogs he befriended during his runaway days. They drop by occasionally, bark at each other for a while, and leave after some time. He never allowed crows or other small animals, such as cats, to venture anywhere near the compound. He would bark to alert us whenever he spotted one. Strangely enough, though, he never barked at strangers.

Now, at almost 11 months old, he has grown into what looks like a fully grown adult dog. A couple of months ago, I got him a cage so he could have a space of his own instead of sleeping under my car.
